Method — Spark Plasma Sintering

  1. 01
    Weigh & Mill

    Weigh V, Zr, Ta, Ca, Zn powders in equimolar (~18 at.%) ratios. Add S and P dopants at 1 at.% each. High-energy ball-mill under Ar atmosphere for 40 h to form a homogenous pre-alloy powder.

  2. 02
    Promethium Doping

    Inside a shielded glove-box, blend Pm₂O₃ powder (1 at.% equivalent) into the pre-alloy. Handle as radioactive material per IAEA safety protocols.

  3. 03
    Load Die & Backfill Ne

    Transfer powder to a graphite SPS die. Evacuate the chamber, then backfill with high-pressure neon gas (≥ 200 MPa). The gas will be mechanically trapped in the powder bed.

  4. 04
    Spark Plasma Sinter

    Apply 50 kA pulsed DC current + 200 MPa uniaxial pressure. Ramp to 1400 °C in < 5 min. Hold 10 min. Rapid sintering forces neon into closed-pore nanobubbles (2–10 nm) before they can escape.

  5. 05
    Cool & Characterise

    Natural cool to room temperature under pressure. Section samples for XRD (confirm BCC + minor precipitate phases), TEM (image Ne nanobubbles), and micro-hardness testing.

Method — Cold Isostatic Pressing + Sintering

  1. 01
    Co-precipitation of Oxides

    Dissolve nitrate salts of Ba, K, Al, Zr, Ca in stoichiometric ratios. Coprecipitate with ammonium hydroxide. Collect, wash, and calcine at 600 °C to form a mixed-oxide nanopowder.

  2. 02
    Dopant Blending

    Blend Pm₂O₃, As₂O₃, and ZnO dopants (1 at.% each) into the base powder by planetary milling. Work As₂O₃ in a ventilated fume hood; handle Pm under radiation protocols.

  3. 03
    Cold Isostatic Pressing

    Encapsulate powder in a flexible mold. Apply 300 MPa isostatic cold pressure using a high-pressure fluid medium. The uniform pressure pre-densifies the powder and introduces a controlled internal stress field.

  4. 04
    High-Temperature Sintering

    Fire the green compact at 1300–1500 °C in an O₂-rich atmosphere for 4 h. Multiple cations force formation of a single high-entropy oxide phase, locking in the stressed lattice.

  5. 05
    Pole & Characterise

    Apply a strong DC field at elevated temperature to "pole" the piezoelectric domains into alignment. Test d₃₃ piezoelectric coefficient, dielectric permittivity, and impedance spectroscopy.

The Science Behind High Entropy

🌀

Configurational Entropy

With 5+ elements in equimolar proportions, the mixing entropy (ΔSmix ≥ 1.5R) overwhelms the enthalpy of competing phases, forcing a single stable solid solution instead of brittle intermetallics.

🏗️

Lattice Distortion

Atoms of different sizes packed into the same crystal sites create a "cocktail" of local strains. These strain fields scatter dislocations — the very defects that cause metals to weaken — dramatically boosting hardness.

🐢

Sluggish Diffusion

In a maze of different neighbours, atoms diffuse far more slowly than in conventional alloys. This "sluggish diffusion" effect keeps the microstructure stable at temperatures that would cause ordinary metals to recrystallise.

🎭

Cocktail Effect

The overall properties are not a simple average of the constituents — synergistic interactions create emergent effects like the Ne nanobubble radiation shielding or the piezoelectric response in the stressed oxide ceramic.
